## Product contract for release one

- Native, itemized estimates associated with a workspace, customer, authorized signing contact, optional opportunity and optional project. Require a project association before collecting a production payment; create/link it idempotently if estimating begins before a project exists.
- Private costs, selling prices, markup, gross margin and profit; estimated payment fees during quoting and reconciled actual fees during collection. Unknown costs remain unknown rather than zero.
- Versioned proposals with scope, materials, exclusions, shipping/site/billing address, tax treatment, expiration, design snapshot, schedule, terms and signature evidence. A shared revision is immutable.
- Customer journey: secure invitation → review estimate/design → approve scope and price → accept terms and electronic-record disclosures → e-sign → pay deposit → receipt. No customer account, password or FTOPS membership required. Signer verification may use an emailed one-time code.
- Major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, subject to approved merchant capabilities) and ACH from day one. Mobile card checkout is the primary path, with clear bank-payment choice. Hosted checkout/hosted fields collect all card and bank details directly at the provider. Wallets are useful where supported, but cannot substitute for ordinary card entry.
- Fixed or percentage deposits, milestone payment requests and final balances. Freeze all resulting cents in the agreement; reconcile installment sums exactly. Clearly show processing vs payment confirmed vs bank settlement. Opening checkout or returning to a success page never means paid.
- Track processing fees, payout/settlement status, full/partial refunds, disputes, chargebacks, ACH returns and fee reversals from release one. Show the customer an accurate balance and staff an actionable exception queue.
- Provider-independent domain records and payment adapter boundaries. From Trees is the merchant; prefer settlement into its own bank account. No FTOPS-held funds or platform wallet requirement.
- Never store, transmit through the FTOPS server, log or include in analytics raw PAN/CVC, routing/account numbers or online-bank credentials. Store provider references and permitted masked descriptors only. Exclude hosted payment fields from session replay.

## Ownership and proposed storage

FTOPS owns commercial revisions, agreements, schedules, invoices, payment allocations and operational readiness. Cabinitron owns design authoring and pricing configuration. The payment processor owns payment instruments and execution. FTOPS is the operational and commercial source of truth. QuickBooks has no role in creating, issuing, approving, signing, collecting, refunding, reconciling or reporting native transactions. No QuickBooks account, identifiers, credentials, API calls or synchronized records are required. A future accounting export/sync, if requested, is optional and downstream only; the destination may be QuickBooks or another tool.

## State and concurrency

Revision lifecycle: `draft → issued → accepted` with `declined`, `expired`, `withdrawn`, `superseded` alternatives. An agreement separately tracks `unsigned → signing → signed` or `voided`. Payment requests track `due → processing → paid`, plus failures/returns/refunds/disputes. Payout state is separate: a succeeded payment can still await bank settlement and remain subject to reversal.

Issue and accept with compare-and-swap against the current revision/status/hash, using a D1 atomic batch and uniqueness constraints. Reject stale tabs, withdrawn links, expired offers and repeated conflicting requests. Accepting price alone does not authorize a debit. E-signing alone does not mean deposit paid. Production readiness requires the configured signed-agreement and verified-payment policy; ACH processing must not silently satisfy it.

Freeze document bytes, itemization, tax, schedule, terms, design and rates at issue. Edits create a new revision and revoke superseded grants/checkout attempts. Block edits affecting a processing payment; reconcile it before amendment. Preserve existing signed agreements, payments and audit history. After signing use explicit change orders and additional signatures; never mutate the executed agreement. Record both design approval and price/terms approval even if the customer completes them in one session.

## Arithmetic, tax and profitability

The first implementation is `apps/api/src/domain/estimate.ts`, with tests in `apps/api/test/unit/estimate.test.ts`. It is a pure library, not an exposed endpoint. It supports USD cents, integer thousandths of a unit, line discounts, caller-supplied parts-per-million tax rates, half-up line rounding, fixed/percentage deposits, fixed/percentage milestone schedules, final-balance reconciliation, private economics and an explicit customer allowlist.

Markup = profit / cost; margin = profit / net selling revenue. Tax is excluded from profit. Unknown line cost makes aggregate profit unknown. `priceWithMarkup` and `priceForMargin` are distinct rounded pricing suggestions. The calculation version must be stored on future revisions. Percentages use the full approved tax-inclusive total for installments; final balance absorbs rounding. No default deposit percentage or tax rate is imposed.

`projectProfitability` subtracts processing/refund/dispute fees from net revenue less recorded project cost. Revenue is exclusive of tax and net of recognized credits/refunds/losses. Keep quoting estimates and actual costs/fees separately; do not treat an incomplete project as final profit. Reconcile fee reversals before passing net expenses. A pending dispute withdrawal affects cash exposure; it must not automatically become both a revenue loss and an expense. Allocate shared payout/FX/expedite fees explicitly and show unallocated expenses. A withheld reserve delays cash but is not itself a processing expense. Fee estimation must follow the chosen card mix and installment count, including per-transaction charges; actual fee ledger entries replace estimates rather than adding to them.

This library does not determine taxability, destination sourcing, exemptions, nexus, registrations or remittance. Tax policy/provider must resolve line classifications and address-based jurisdiction components. Distinguish goods/fabrication, delivery and installation; do not blanket-exempt labor. Save address, exemption evidence, rate components, effective date, calculation reference and reviewer. If final tax differs from the accepted total, require a revised agreement or a counsel-approved disclosed adjustment process before charging. Prevent charging tax twice across deposit and final invoices; a reviewed accounting policy determines tax recognition/allocation within FTOPS, independently of any external accounting software.

California furniture fabrication and construction-contract tax treatment differ: see [CDTFA merchandise/fabrication guidance](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub35/designer-fees-and-charges-related-to-the-sale-of-merchandise.htm) and [construction contractor guidance](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/construction-contractors/industry-topics.htm). The draft calculator's line rounding must be reconciled with the selected tax engine before production.

## Cabinitron import contract

Create a server-authorized integration mapping `(workspace, environment) → Cabinitron business`, configured by an authorized administrator and confirmed by the business owner. The browser cannot nominate an arbitrary tenant or supply shared staff cookies. Add a dedicated narrowly scoped machine credential/service binding or delegated integration grant in a separately reviewed Cabinitron Artifacts diff. Do not reuse the H2 reporting token.

Request a saved design slug and exact revision. Return a structured versioned snapshot: business, design revision, pricing configuration revision/hash, currency, stable cabinet/element IDs, quantities, dimensions/selections, cost and selling components, exclusions, assumptions, rendered/plan artifact hashes. Preserve explicit unpriced items. Do not import a customer price range as a fixed quote. The current owner/admin cost-report can inform this contract but is not an existing machine-to-machine authorization mechanism.

Freeze the actual pricing configuration/input bytes or an immutable rates version, not just `ratesUpdatedAt`. Read design and rates coherently; reject a stale revision or concurrent pricing change. Import idempotency uses workspace + business + design revision + pricing hash + import intent. Reimports show a staff-reviewed diff and create a new estimate revision, never overwrite accepted lines. Keep stable source IDs and distinguish attached finish panels already included under their cabinet. Reconcile imported line costs/selling totals to the source snapshot before issue. Group by room only when an explicit cabinet-to-room map reconciles. Never copy edit keys, private source JSON, internal CSVs or cost reports into customer downloads/signature envelopes.

## Guest contracting and defensible signatures

Use a separate public Worker/origin or explicit narrowly scoped public routing. The current staff Access application/proxy cannot simply be made public. Random single-purpose invitation secrets are hashed at rest, scoped to workspace/recipient/revision and actions, expiring/revocable and exchanged for HttpOnly secure sessions. Redact tokens from URLs/logs, use no-store and no-referrer, no indexing/third-party analytics, rate limits and CSRF/origin checks. Account-free means no registration, not no verification.

Before signing, display the exact estimate, scope/design attachments, terms, cancellation/refund provisions, schedule and disclosures. Capture affirmative consent to electronic records separately from agreement acceptance and debit mandates, explicit intent to sign, signer identity/authority, revision hash, timestamps, verification events and an immutable executed copy. Deliver a retainable copy to each party and record delivery. A typed/drawn signature image by itself is insufficient evidence design. IP/user agent, if counsel approves retention, are supporting evidence rather than identity proof.

[ESIGN §7001](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/pdf/USCODE-2024-title15-chap96-subchapI-sec7001.pdf) addresses electronic validity, required consumer disclosures/consent and retention. Have counsel review California UETA, applicable exclusions, consumer disclosures, cancellation notices, delivery, retention, signer attribution and counter-signatures before release. Choose either a reviewed native evidence flow or an embedded signature provider; a provider certificate alone does not resolve contract content or transaction classification.

Treat custom furniture manufacturing/goods as such; do not automatically classify every sale as home improvement. Where installation facts bring a contract under California home-improvement rules, use reviewed contract and deposit policy: [CSLB](https://cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/What_Is_A_Contract.aspx) describes the $1,000/10% down-payment ceiling, whichever is less. Do not encode a universal furniture deposit rule from that guidance. Contract classification must be resolved before issuing payment requests.

## Payment provider boundary and lifecycle

Adapter interface: `capabilities`, `createCheckout`, `retrievePayment`, `verifyAndNormalizeEvent`, `refund`, `retrieveRefund`, `retrieveDispute`, `retrieveFees`, `listPayoutTransactions`, `retrievePayout`. Keep raw provider objects inside the adapter; return normalized IDs, money, method, state, evidence references and timestamps. Credentials are encrypted through the workspace integration pattern and partitioned by provider account/environment. Initial production needs one approved adapter supporting BOTH cards and ACH; modularity does not require operating multiple processors at launch.

Persist a payment attempt before provider creation. Use an obligation/attempt-specific idempotency key; on timeout retry/retrieve the same attempt before creating another. Amount, currency, merchant account and signed obligation come from the server. Avoid private design/cost/terms payloads in provider metadata. No charging after a signed total changes, and no unapproved automated retries of ACH mandates.

Verified, deduplicated webhooks drive state; use raw bytes for verification, then normalized/redacted durable events. Retrieve provider state for out-of-order events and reconcile missed events on a schedule. Check account, environment, cents/currency and signed agreement/request association. Atomically allocate a successful payment and write outbox receipt/activity events. The customer success redirect polls authoritative state. ACH requires provider-hosted verification/mandate, pending/failure/return handling and refund rules; pending transfers reserve the obligation to prevent a duplicate card payment. Expire/revoke the old attempt or reconcile settlement before switching methods. Preserve unexpected duplicate/late receipts as explicit unapplied cash for resolution, not an extra invoice allocation.

Refunds require staff authorization, reason, available refundable amount, provider idempotency and subsequent reconciliation. Preserve partial refunds, fees not returned, disputes/chargebacks and ACH returns with a compensating ledger rather than editing historical payments. Release-one UI shows dispute deadlines and evidence export, payout holds and failed payouts. Never label a payout initiated as funds received at the bank.

Bank-transfer-only, card-only, manual-payment-only or signature-only is not an acceptable initial production release. Optional second processors, advanced analytics and extra wallets may follow; mandatory lifecycle tracking may not.

Project task materialization must be idempotent and must use a workspace-safe public commercial projection only after agreement/payment readiness, not simply on estimate approval.

Optional downstream accounting integration is explicitly outside the initial implementation and release requirements. If requested later, export FTOPS-owned invoices, payments, credits, fees and tax summaries through an isolated adapter with stable IDs, idempotency, delivery status and retry controls. Export failures must not block or change native customer balances, agreements, payments, project readiness or financial reports. External acknowledgments identify exported records only; downstream webhooks must not author or overwrite native commercial records. Historical imported records remain identifiable and separate from native balances to prevent double counting. A separate migration/retirement plan is required before removing existing QuickBooks integration code or historical records; this architecture revision does not disconnect an existing account or delete data.
